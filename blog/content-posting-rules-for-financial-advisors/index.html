Content Posting Rules for Financial Advisors

Fritts Causby  ·  Copacetic Marketing  ·  2 min read

Financial advisors can post on LinkedIn and publish newsletters, but there are strict rules pertaining to the content. This summary covers the basics. It is general information, not compliance advice, and the policies of each firm come first.

Registered investment advisers answer to the SEC and its Marketing Rule. Broker-dealers and their registered representatives answer to FINRA, primarily Rule 2210 on communications with the public. Many firms maintain compliance with both entities.

Retail communications from broker-dealers have traditionally required approval by a registered principal before use. FINRA proposed a more flexible, risk-based approach in 2026, so the current procedure of the firm is the one to follow. Advisers under the SEC rule rely on written policies.

The content should be fair and balanced. Posts should not promise returns, imply guarantees or present benefits without the related risks.

Testimonials and endorsements are permitted in some circumstances, but there are strict disclosure requirements. A best practice is to ask the compliance department for approval.

Business communications, including social media posts and newsletters, generally must be archived.

Marketing teams should be aware that sharing or endorsing a post written by someone else can make the advisor responsible for that content for compliance purposes.
