Press releases and content for medical and dental practices

Businesses that can benefit from copywriting and distribution services include physicians and dentists, group practices, hospitals and outpatient centers, physical therapy clinics, and medical spas/aesthetics practices.

News-worthy events

  • A new provider joins the practice
  • A new location or expanded office
  • New technology or a new service
  • Awards and recognition
  • Community health events and charity sponsorships

Whenever a new provider joins a practice, a new location is opened or a new service is offered, it creates an opportunity for marketing. A press release could be centered on new technology, awards and recognition, to add credibility to the organization. Community health events and charity partnerships can be particularly impactful for enhancing the reputation of the business.

What is included

A press release that is customized to the practice or provider that highlights the unique value proposition of the business. This includes industry research, stakeholder interviews and up to four edits to ensure accuracy. Outreach and distribution to local media outlets is available as an add-on purchase.

Practices can also add provider bios, service pages for the practice website, blog posts and LinkedIn content written from the perspective of each physician or dentist. Larger projects can grow into omnichannel campaigns, with one story carried through press coverage, email newsletters, social media and the website. AI tools help with research and measuring results, and every word is written and reviewed by a person.

Rules that apply

Healthcare marketing answers to several sets of rules. Under HIPAA, a patient story, photo or testimonial that includes protected health information needs the patient’s written authorization, and the authorization must explain how to revoke it. The Federal Trade Commission requires health claims to be truthful and backed by competent and reliable evidence, so phrases like “guaranteed results” are prohibited. Claims about drugs, devices and supplements also fall under FDA rules, which matters most for medical spas and aesthetics practices. State medical and dental boards often have separate advertising rules based on words such as “specialist,” “board certified” and “expert.” Every draft goes to the practice for review against its compliance policies, and nothing on this page constitutes legal advice.
