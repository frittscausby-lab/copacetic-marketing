Copywriting for builders, contractors and developers

Builders, general contractors, developers, architects and engineering firms need press releases, project narratives, website copy and help with the written sections of proposals.

News-worthy events

  • Contract wins
  • Groundbreakings and ribbon cuttings
  • Project completions
  • New hires and promotions
  • Safety and industry awards
  • Topping-out ceremonies and other construction milestones
  • Green building certifications, such as LEED
  • Community involvement, such as volunteer builds and sponsorships

Whenever a contractor, builder or developer has a news-worthy event, such as a contract win, a new hire or an award, it is an opportunity to build awareness about the business. Maintaining the pipeline of new work requires consistency, and press releases can be an effective means of achieving that goal. Other services include assisting with announcements regarding the completion of high-profile projects, website copy, and support with Requests for Proposals (RFPs).

Milestones also make news. A topping-out ceremony, a green building certification or a volunteer build gives editors a reason to cover the firm between major projects, and each release adds to a public record that prospective clients can find when they research the firm.

What is included

A press release customized to the firm and the project, with research on the project and the community it serves, interviews with the owner, the architect or the project manager, along with up to four edits to ensure accuracy. Outreach to local business editors and trade publications is available as an add-on. Firms can also add project narratives, website copy and support with the written sections of RFP responses. Larger projects can grow into omnichannel campaigns, with one story carried through press coverage, email newsletters, social media and the website. AI tools help with research and measuring results, and every word is written and reviewed by a person.

Photo captions, a short version for social media and a version for the company newsletter can be written alongside the release, so one announcement covers every channel.

Rules that apply

Many states require contractors to include a license number in advertising. Some states call for license numbers on all contracts, advertisements and websites, and California requires the number in print, broadcast and online ads. A release or web page should carry the number where the state requires it. Safety records, award names and project costs are confirmed in writing before publication, and details from an RFP stay confidential unless the use of the details is specifically approved by the client. Each firm should confirm the rules of the state in which it operates. Nothing on this page constitutes legal advice.
