How to Start Working with a Ghostwriter

Fritts Causby  ·  Copacetic Marketing  ·  2 min read

The first conversation with a ghostwriter produces a better outcome when the client brings a few specific ideas. A consistent piece of advice I give to my clients is not to worry about providing too much information. It is truly the more information the better, as it helps me form a more complete picture of the business.

The first objective is to identify the goal of the article. A post aimed at earning referrals from colleagues or advancing recruitment efforts is different from an article meant to reassure a first-time homebuyer. The target audience and the action that is desired from the reader sets the tone before a word is drafted.

The information that is provided is essential for creating an article from the perspective of the client. This could include bullet points in an email, a transcript, industry article or web page. A ghostwriter analyzes the information for sentence length, formality and tone. A recorded conversation can be the entire basis for an article if that is the preference of the client.

Of course, the material forms the basis of the article. Whether it’s a slide deck, meeting notes or just a list of points to make, the facts are vital for guiding the writer. Being specific about the tone, details and direction of the article helps to reduce the number of edits that may be required.

The platform also has a significant impact on the finished product. A newsletter, LinkedIn post, a press release and a website page each have a different set of requirements and conventions, and a piece that was written for a specific platform rarely transfers without reworking.

Deadlines and the expected number of revisions should be included in the initial conversation. It is also helpful to discuss anything that should be excluded, such as proprietary information, trade secrets, pending legal matters, or business strategies. A prudent ghostwriter will ask, but it saves time when the client volunteers.

An email or a short conversation is usually enough to get started, however. The rest can follow as the work settles into a rhythm. I have extensive experience working with clients in real estate, law, finance and technical fields, though I welcome new clients and the research, interview and writing skills are applicable to every industry.
