Why Top Agents Hire a Real Estate Writer

Fritts Causby  ·  Copacetic Marketing  ·  2 min read

The agents at the top of a market have two things in common: a constant need for written material and very little time available in front of a computer. Production in residential real estate depends on relationships, and relationships are built at listing appointments, inspections, broker caravans and client dinners rather than in front of a keyboard. The writing still needs to be done, which is why the most productive agents hand it to a professional.

A bio is the first piece that many agents choose to outsource. Prospective clients research an agent before the first meeting, and the bio is usually the first thing they read. A bio that lists production figures and designations tells a reader what the agent has done. A bio that explains how the agent works and what a client can expect from the first call tells the reader whether to pick up the phone. A bio should also carry personal elements, because people want to work with someone they can relate to and qualifications vary.

Property descriptions carry more weight than the length would suggest. A seller reads the description of the family home closely and may want to share it with their network. A description that is specific to the house, the street and the surrounding neighborhood shows that the agent invested time and effort into the listing. Buyers want clarity, and the type of information that only the sellers or a local expert would know. A description assembled from the same adjectives as every other listing in the MLS signals the opposite. Sellers notice and negative feedback about an agent can spread quickly.

Market updates, newsletters and process guides build engagement. An agent who sends a monthly note on local inventory and pricing stays in front of past clients, generating a higher potential for referrals. Producing a knowledge-based article means pulling statistics and providing insights on those statistics, and that is time that most agents do not have.

At the brokerage level, a press release can be more effective than an advertisement. Agent awards, new offices, fundraisers and unusual listings are legitimate news to a local business journal or trade outlet. A press release carries more credibility than an advertisement and is also more cost effective.

Agents like to share information with their clients and sphere of influence. Since a press release contains more detail than an advertisement, sharing it is more worthwhile. When agents read a press release about a colleague, it can enhance recruitment efforts put forth by the brokerage.

In the upper tier of the market, the stakes are always higher. Buyers and sellers of luxury homes tend to be highly educated and well connected. Considering the description of an estate may be forwarded among friends, advisors and members of the press, it is easy to see why it should be taken seriously.

For examples of narratives about architecturally significant homes, see the Ray Kappe House and Villa Maggio, the last intact estate of Frank Sinatra on The Pinnacle, and a coastal home feature on frittscausby.com.

The quality of the writing becomes part of the presentation, and the fee for hiring a writer is a small fraction of the commission.
