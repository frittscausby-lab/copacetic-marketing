The Role of a LinkedIn Ghostwriter

Fritts Causby  ·  Copacetic Marketing  ·  2 min read

A managing partner understands the competitive advantage that can be realized from a steady presence on LinkedIn. Business owners and leaders know where their industries are headed better than anyone and, in most cases, they are long on ideas and short on time.

A LinkedIn ghostwriter closes that gap. Depending on the time constraints of the client, the work can begin from a document shared through a notes app, an email or a short, recorded conversation. The writer asks about recent industry developments, the questions that are often raised by clients, the economic trends affecting the practice, and the particular value the firm offers. The answers become the raw material.

From that material, the ghostwriter develops content from the perspective of the client. This includes using the client’s tone, vocabulary, idioms and phrases. A post for a litigator should read differently from a post for a trusts and estates attorney, and both should have a different tone than a post by a real estate broker. The ghostwriter's job is to notice and preserve those differences.

The client reviews each draft before publishing. Drafts are not written in stone, changes are welcome and the appearance of the final version is ultimately up to the client. Review tends to go quickly because the draft is built from the client's ideas instead of a template.

A professional ghostwriter understands how to be discreet when it is necessary. Confidentiality, trade secrets, specific details and any potential discussion that could have a negative impact on the reputation of the client are not included in the article. For attorneys, state bar rules govern what may be said and how it is presented, and the firm's review is the final word.

The result is a consistent presence that reflects real expertise. Connections see the perspective of the client/owner week after week and the level of trust/credibility accumulates.

My work as a ghostwriter has included developing sponsored content for a variety of businesses for the Wilmington Business Journal and ongoing legal content for Ward and Smith, a regional law firm. LinkedIn is the same craft in a shorter form: listen closely, write clearly and let the expert take the credit.
