Content for heating and air conditioning companies

Blog posts, newsletters and sponsored articles help heating and air conditioning companies explain their services to homeowners and stay top of mind between service calls. The writing turns technical topics, such as zoning, humidity and indoor air quality, into articles that homeowners actually read.

News-worthy events

  • Seasonal maintenance reminders
  • New equipment and technology
  • Maintenance plans and promotions
  • New technicians and service areas
  • Awards and certifications
  • Community sponsorships

Every season gives an HVAC company a reason to reach out, from spring allergy tips to fall heating checkups. New equipment, such as ductless mini-splits or high-efficiency heat pumps, is worth explaining before a homeowner needs it. A maintenance plan or a seasonal promotion gives readers a reason to call. New service areas, awards and community sponsorships are valid reasons for a press release.

What is included

Available services include blog posts, newsletters, sponsored articles and press releases, each written from a short interview and online research. Up to four edits are provided. Technical topics are explained in plain language, and each article can be reused on a website, in an email and on social media.

Larger projects can grow into omnichannel campaigns, with one story carried through press coverage, email newsletters, social media and the website. AI tools help with research and measuring results, and every word is written and reviewed by a person.

Ghostwriting services

Monthly content subscription: one ready-to-send article a month

Rules that apply

Many states require HVAC contractors to include a license number in advertising. Claims about energy savings, efficiency ratings and indoor air quality should be accurate and supported, since the Federal Trade Commission treats unsupported savings and health claims as deceptive. Efficiency ratings follow the current Department of Energy standards, and rebates or tax credits are confirmed for the current year, since federal and utility programs change. Each company should confirm the rules of the state in which it operates. Nothing on this page constitutes legal advice.
