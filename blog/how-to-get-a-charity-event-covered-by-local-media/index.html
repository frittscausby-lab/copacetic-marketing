How to Get a Charity Event Covered by Local Media

Fritts Causby  ·  Copacetic Marketing  ·  2 min read

Local editors receive more event announcements than they can publish, yet charity events remain among the stories they are most willing to run. The difference between a release that is published and one that is ignored usually comes down to timing, focus and a few details that are easy to overlook.

Timing is paramount, of course. Community calendars and weekly publications often need information two to four weeks in advance. A short release at that point announces the event, and a second release afterward reports the results. Editors are more likely to publish the second release when it includes a specific figure, such as the amount that was raised or the number of families that were served.

The headline and first paragraph should state what is happening, where, when and for whom. The cause belongs in the opening, not in the last paragraph. A reader who only reads the first two sentences should understand why the event matters.

A quote from a person adds weight. The best quotes describe a specific result, such as the impact of the funds that were raised last year and/or how a program was positively impactful for a local family, shared with their permission.

Photos can dramatically increase the likelihood of getting coverage. High-resolution images from past events give an editor something to publish immediately. Of course, it is advisable to get the names and permission of the people who appear in the photos.

Finally, the release should go to the right person. A short, personal email to the pertinent editor or reporter who covers community news is more effective than a mass distribution. A follow-up a few days later is appropriate; repeated follow-ups are not.
