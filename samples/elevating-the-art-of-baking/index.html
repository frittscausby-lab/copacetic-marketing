Elevating the Art of Baking: The Artistry of Hans Westermark

This is a warning: do not search for the work of Hans Westermark on social media with the idea that it will only take a little bit of time. To put it simply, the master baker’s point-of-view style videos showing the artistry of his work decorating cakes and other mouthwatering delectables for Apple Annie’s Bake Shop have a way of drawing the viewer in, making time and other worries melt away.

“Surprisingly enough, I have a lot of older followers who are over 30,” said Westermark, whose supporters worldwide were in excess of 375,000 at the time of this writing. “It’s not uncommon for people to come in and ask me to reproduce something that they saw on one of my videos.”

Real-time videos that show the placement/inclusion of intricate rosettes, flowers, animals and landscapes are just a few examples of what can be found on Westermark’s feed. A particularly click-worthy, fun distraction is the cake that he created to celebrate the 70th anniversary of the Harbor Island Garden Club, as it displays the incredible depth and range of his talent.

Westermark says his decision to integrate music corresponding with each video was the secret ingredient that allowed him to gain more traction on the platform. “The first few videos were pretty bad, with no music, you can hear noises in the background and the crunching of the icing bags,” said Westermark.

Now, he picks out a song to go with the unique feeling, vibe or theme for each cake. After watching the video for the Garden Club dessert, which has The Cowsills 1967-hit The Rain The Park and Other Things as a backdrop, it’s hard to imagine a better pairing. Some other examples of the music Westermark has used to showcase his creations include Amie by Pure Prairie League, Does Your Mother Know by ABBA, and Hypnotized by Fleetwood Mac, a song that he included in a response video to a customer’s question of whether he or not he had the capability to write happy birthday on a cupcake.

“I literally write happy birthday hundreds of times a day,” said Westermark, who, amazingly, records all the videos while holding his phone in his left hand. With a mother that was a Suzuki-trained violinist, he is well aware of the notion that is not possible to become an expert at something until it has been practiced 10,000 times.

Raised by a family of bakers, Westermark traveled to Pennsylvania during the summer of 1990 to work in his grandfather’s bakery. “I didn’t realize this was going to be the start of the rest of my life,” he said.

From there Westermark returned home to finish high school and got a job at a grocery store, but it didn’t work out, so he applied at Apple Annie’s. Fearing that his artwork would be ruined if he left it in the car while working in the position he received, which mainly involved cleaning up, Westermark started bringing it inside. The rest, as they say, is history, as the owner recognized his talent, along with his work ethic and experience working for his grandfather, so he asked him to become an apprentice.

After seven years at the bake shop, Westermark did an exchange program in Switzerland for 18 months to learn the Swiss way of baking. He returned to Wilmington in 1999 with an entirely new set of skills, which he successfully integrated into the products.

For the first 20 years, Westermark worked side-by-side with the owner, Gary Longordo, decorating cakes. After Longordo sold the business to a new owner, Westermark became the only cake decorator, a position he’s held for the past 10 years.

“I’ve learned a lot because I like to experiment a lot,” said Westermark, who has been featured in both national and international media outlets. “My style is very old school apparently, partially because I use parchment for decorating instead of pre-formed plastic bags.”

It is in keeping with the eco-friendly business philosophy at Apple Annie’s. To reduce their environmental footprint, the bakery donates unsold items to food banks, uses local products when possible, reuses and recycles containers from suppliers, and researches all vendors to ensure that items are sourced in a sustainable way.

Whether or not the longevity of employees is a measurement of the way they are treated or the success of a business may be up for debate, however it is worth pointing out that three of Westermark’s fellow employees have been there for decades. Jamy, the head baker, has been working with the business for over 44 years, Greg, who makes the buttercream and many other key items, has been there 33 years, and Nancy, who specializes in customer service, has worked at the bakery for over 25 years.

“Since I started doing these videos, it has seemed like things have gotten a lot busier around here,” said Westermark, “but that may just be from pent-up demand from people wanting to get out more after the pandemic.”

Though Westermark always preferred to be behind the scenes, he is adjusting to living a more public life. “Being recognized on social media has been a breath of fresh air,” said Westermark. “I read all the comments on my page and my followers have given me lots of great ideas.”

Westermark loves the idea that he is keeping the old ways alive and having the opportunity to teach and inspire those who are watching. “It’s wonderful to feel the support of the community and it is extremely validating. I have always loved technology and decorating cakes. This blends everything together and I feel like I’m living in a dream. Since this happened my followers have been my life force to keep on creating,” he said.

The challenge of adequately presenting Westermark’s work on the printed page may be one of the best possible examples of the limitations of the medium. But the argument over whether or not a picture is actually worth 1,000 words quickly fades into the background after viewing the artistry of his work.

And regardless of the criticism surrounding social media in general, Westermark is able to connect with followers around the globe. This provides a break and a distraction from the worries, anxiety and fear that whirl about our daily lives, and what could be sweeter than that?

Published in Wrightsville Beach Magazine, 2022.
