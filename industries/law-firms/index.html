Ghostwriting and press releases for law firms

Long-form content, client alerts, web pages and newsletter articles raise awareness for law firms and attorneys, demonstrating expertise and keeping clients informed. Each piece is written from a short interview and reviewed by the attorney before publication.

News-worthy events

  • New attorneys and partners
  • New practice areas and offices
  • Awards and rankings
  • Case results and transactions, with client consent
  • Seminars, CLEs and speaking engagements
  • Changes in the law that affect clients

A new partner, practice area or office is a valid reason for a press release. Awards and rankings can add credibility; case results and transactions can also make news. Seminars and speaking engagements show expertise, and legal updates provide a reason to publish a client alert or newsletter.

What is included

Available services include blog posts, client alerts, bylined articles, practice area pages, attorney bios and press releases. Information can be obtained from a short interview and online research. Up to four edits are provided. Seminars and conferences can be repurposed into a series of articles through the Conference to Content service.

Larger projects can grow into omnichannel campaigns, with one story carried through press coverage, email newsletters, social media and the website. AI tools help with research and measuring results, and every word is written and reviewed by a person.

Rules that apply

Attorney advertising follows the rules of professional conduct in each state, and most are based on the ABA Model Rules. Communications cannot be false or misleading, and a case result can create an unjustified expectation without context, so many states call for a disclaimer. An attorney cannot claim to be a certified specialist unless an approved organization has granted the certification. Some states require certain communications to be labeled as advertising or filed with the state bar. Client names and case details are used only with the consent of the client, and awards are described according to the rules of the state. Ghostwritten content is published under the name of the attorney, who reviews and approves every piece. Each firm should confirm the rules of the state in which it practices.
