What to Include in a Groundbreaking or Ribbon-Cutting Press Release

Fritts Causby  ·  Copacetic Marketing  ·  2 min read

A groundbreaking or a ribbon cutting is one of the few moments when a construction project becomes a news-worthy event. Having an accurate, well-written press release ready to go increases the probability of getting coverage from local publications.

The headline should name the project, location and milestone. A reader should know within a few words if a new medical office broke ground or a renovated library re-opened its doors, for example. The first paragraph, of course, is meant to address the key points: what was built or will be built, where it is, who is involved and when the event takes place.

The second paragraph explains why the project matters to the community. Editors will want to know how many jobs were created, the services that will operate in the building, and the problem the project solves. A quote from the owner and/or a local official with specific details about the project should also be included.

Credit belongs to the partners that want it, whether it is the owner, general contractor, architect, lenders or subcontractors. Everyone named in the release should have an opportunity to approve the wording before publication, and every figure should be confirmed by the party responsible for it. Where state law requires a license number in advertising, the release should include that information.

Significantly, if an article is written by a reporter at a local publication, the people named in the article will not have an opportunity to review or make changes before publication. A press release written by the firm is the best way to ensure the details are right.

Photos add detail and increase engagement. A good rule of thumb is to offer a few different options, such as a wide shot of the ceremony or a rendering of the finished project. Captions should name the people in each photo from left to right.

For a groundbreaking, the release can go out a week ahead as a media advisory and again the day of the event. For a ribbon cutting, a release could be distributed to editors the same afternoon, so they have what they need for a same-day or next-day story. A copy posted on the company website keeps the announcement working after the news cycle is over.
