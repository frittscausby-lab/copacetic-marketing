Garden Club and Hobby Shop Make a Retirement Community Unique

This June we are hosting an open house to showcase our Garden Club and Hobby Shop, two amenities that consistently top the list of favorites for our residents. Both are completely free for the residents to use and each one exemplifies the community’s focus on creating a comfortable, pleasant environment.

“It’s the people here that make the difference,” said Tom, Director of the Garden Club. “Everyone here had such a diverse and interesting career. You never know who you will meet when you go to dinner.”

Tom, who taught at a university, is explaining why he and his wife Ann decided to relocate here after spending 44 years in the Washington, D.C. area. “We are truly one big, happy family,” he adds, settling into a chair underneath an umbrella.

Tom helps in keeping the Garden Club’s 24 active members organized and ensures adequate supplies are on hand. He said one of his chief concerns in leaving his home for a Life Plan Community was having outdoor space to roam and a place to get his hands dirty in the garden. “Now all I have to worry about is what to do with all these tomatoes, blueberries, potatoes and cucumbers,” he joked.

This concern about having outdoor space to plant flowers and vegetables is the reason why the Garden Club was first established and why it continues to bear the name of the resident who petitioned the board to set aside a place for the residents 10 years ago. With status as a nonprofit, the board is always looking for new ways to reinvest the community’s earnings back into refining the services and amenities it provides for its residents.

As a result, there is now a spacious, wheelchair-accessible garden area that is set on approximately a half acre of land. It is completely surrounded by a privacy fence to ward off deer and other critters attracted by the aroma of the sweet smelling flowers and vegetables. A variety of plot sizes are available, from 20’ by 20’ and 10’ by 20’ ground plots, to raised beds and table gardens, for those who have trouble squatting or spending extensive periods of time on their knees.

Tom is considering polling the members about dividing the 20’ by 20’ plots. “They are almost too big,” he laughs. “And we want to make sure there is space for everyone.” Each of the plots has access to running water and a shed for storing tools. There are even two large compost bins for the residents to share organic fertilizer. The grounds and maintenance department helps with weed control and mulching, and operations are facilitated by a memorial fund specifically set aside for the Garden Club.

A quick stroll through the garden and it’s easy to see why so many works of art were inspired by the beauty put on display by the natural world. One of those unique artists with a talent for replicating the complexity found in nature is resident Walt, who can often be found in the woodworking and hobby shop, whittling away at a block of wood. Looking at a shell with tinges of red that he recently carved from a box elder tree, it is surprising to learn the red tones are actually produced by a stress reaction the trees have to a specific type of fungus. All of Walt’s works have a shine that looks as if he’s applied a protective coat of polyurethane. In reality this comes from many, many hours of his time sanding the wood, until he finishes with a 12,000 grit sand paper and calls it done.

Fellow Craftsmen Hank and Ray are equally talented. Ray is the go-to guy in the community when furniture needs to be repaired, refurbished or simply created from scratch to the highest standards of quality and style. Hank is the artisan responsible for crafting some really intricate and cool nut crackers, which take about 20 to 25 hours each to produce.

What the guys have in common is a focus on helping others, so each piece is either donated to a local church or sold in the gift shop at a nearby care center to fund the operations there. Another common element is an appreciation for the simple fact the shop is there and filled with every tool imaginable. A relief in not having to leave anything behind is a bond they share with the members of the Garden Club. Owning a home takes work. Part of the reward is the freedom to have a garden or a workshop. Here, we constantly look for new ways to ensure our residents have access to all the comforts of home, plus world-class health care and resort-like amenities, along with the sense of being free from the drudgery of interior and exterior home maintenance.
