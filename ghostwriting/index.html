Ghostwriting

Ghostwriting Services

Articles, posts and newsletters built from a short conversation. The client supplies the expertise, and I supply the writing.

Professionals know their fields better than anyone writing about them, yet few have the time to turn that knowledge into a steady flow of content. Working with a ghostwriter reduces the time it takes to produce steady, publishable content. A short interview establishes the ideas, I draft from the perspective of the client, and nothing is published under the client's name until it has been reviewed and approved. The information can be shared in the form that is the best fit for the needs of the client, whether it is a transcript from a notes app, a phone call or a link to an existing website. I work around the client's schedule and make the process as easy as possible.

For attorneys

Blog posts, client alerts, bylined articles and practice area pages from the perspective of the attorney, with answers to the questions clients ask. I have written legal content for Ward and Smith, P.A. since 2021. I also attend firm seminars and conferences in person and turn the sessions into articles. Professional rules vary by state, so the attorney should confirm that the final piece meets the applicable obligations. Anyone can ask a chatbot for complicated legal jargon that will put readers to sleep. I write articles that are accurate, engaging and conversational.

How it works

  1. I research the client and the business before the call, so the 20 to 30 minute conversation is informed and flows naturally instead of following a list of questions. The call is recorded with permission.
  2. I research additional sources, create a draft from the perspective of the client and send it for review.
  3. The client reviews and edits, and the piece is finalized through four rounds of revisions.
  4. I deliver a version ready to publish, with a headline and, if desired, a search-friendly summary.

Turnaround is typically one to two weeks from interview to final.

Questions

Will it sound like me?

That is the purpose of the interview. I write from the conversation, using specific words and examples, then refine the phrasing through four rounds of revisions.

Can a ghostwriter be used in a given profession?

In most cases, yes, with review and appropriate credit, but the rules vary. This is not legal advice, and professional rules should be checked.
