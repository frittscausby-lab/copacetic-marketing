What a Business Gains by Outsourcing Professional Writing

Fritts Causby  ·  Copacetic Marketing  ·  2 min read

Many professional services firms produce and distribute content that was created by someone who does not have the word writer in their job title. A managing partner may write a client alert late at night. A broker-owner may draft a newsletter between showings. An office manager may be asked to write a press release because of having prior experience at a newspaper.

The writing is done at a cost that is difficult to quantify. Judging the quality of the work is unfair to the writer if that person does not specialize in that area of business on a daily basis.

Outsourcing the work to a professional changes the math.

One reason is the hourly rate of the writer is typically lower than an attorney or managing broker, for example. An attorney who bills at $500 per hour and spends a few on an article has created a very expensive article. That is before counting client work that was displaced and/or opportunity costs. If an attorney hires a writer, the involvement that is required drops substantially, down to providing some information and a review of the draft.

A writer who has produced several hundred market updates or client alerts has already worked out the structure readers follow and the mistakes that lose them. A firm producing its first dozen is learning those lessons on the job.

Outsourcing content needs may also be preferable to hiring someone to work on staff. A staff writer's salary and benefits are fixed whether the month is profitable or not. An outside writer is paid for the work that is produced, which is often a better fit for a brokerage with needs that spike around market reports and new listings, or a law firm with content needs that revolve around the latest updates in the legal field.

An outside writer can also make complex ideas more approachable to the average person. An expert who is already in the business may use jargon and terms that are only recognizable to their peers and colleagues. An outside writer asks the questions a prospective client would ask and clearly explains technical ideas, such that the article is easy to read and informative.

For a sample of a business feature written for a general audience, see Bringing it all Together, an article on the headquarters of nCino published in Wrightsville Beach Magazine.

Ongoing engagements allow the writer to make a lasting, positive impact on the organization. A writer can build a thorough knowledge of a client’s business over time, adapting content and style to the strategy, processes and people. The time and effort involved with onboarding is only paid once. As a writer for Berkshire Hathaway HomeServices California Properties since 2008, I have become a trusted resource for the brokerage and the agents it serves.

Outsourcing does not eliminate responsibility for substance or accuracy. The attorney still supplies the analysis, the broker still supplies the market read, and the person whose name is on the piece still reviews every word before it is published. The writer's contribution is the structure, the clarity and time, and a good engagement is honest about that division from the first call.
