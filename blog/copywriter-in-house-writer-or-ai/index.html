Professional Copywriter, In-House Writer, or AI Tool: What a Business Gets from Each

Fritts Causby  ·  Copacetic Marketing  ·  2 min read

A business that needs content can essentially choose from three options: assign it to a person on staff, generate it with AI or hire a professional copywriter. Each produces a different result, of course, and a simple cost/benefit analysis can make it easier to choose between which option supports the needs of the business in the best possible way.

Writing in house has the advantage of proximity. The person doing the work understands the business, can walk down the hall to check a fact and turn a piece around the same day. The downside is that the writing is usually assigned to someone with many other responsibilities, so the work is typically low on the list of priorities. The quality may vary depending on the workflow of the week. Also, an internal writer may use terms and jargon that are difficult for outsiders to understand.

AI tools are faster and cheaper. A tool can produce a competent, grammatical draft on almost any subject in seconds, and for an internal summary or a first pass that might be enough. The limits are clear in a closer review, however. A tool does not interview anyone or brainstorm about additional details that could inform the content, so the finished product does not have new information. The fact that AI will fill gaps with generalities, invent statistics or regulations to support a conclusion is even more concerning. Further, AI takes no responsibility for mistakes and negative outcomes produced by those mistakes. Readers can immediately spot AI-generated material and business may receive a harsh judgment for using it.

A business that chooses a professional copywriter is looking for craft and accountability. The writer begins with research and interviews to uncover new information and details that will be positively impactful to the business. A writer with experience understands the target audience and the importance of researching stated claims. And perhaps most importantly, the reputation of the writer is attached to the result even when the byline belongs to the client. A writer will be more expensive and need more time compared to other options, but for businesses that value quality and accountability, the choice is clear.

Leveraging each option may deliver a competitive advantage for some businesses. Staff handle routine internal communications, tools handle summaries and drafts that outsiders never see, and a professional writer handles the content that could positively or negatively impact the reputation of the business: the website, thought leadership, the press releases, newsletters—basically anything a client or a regulator might read.

My work is mainly focused on quality and accountability over speed, but if you’re looking for someone to help review your AI-generated content, I am open to ideas and the word ‘No’ is not in my vocabulary when it comes to discussing a project with a client.
